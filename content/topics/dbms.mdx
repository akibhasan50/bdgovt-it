## ER model essentials

Entities, attributes, relationships (1:1, 1:N, M:N). Weak entity → dependent on a key of another entity, identified by partial key + owner. A **ternary relationship** connects three entity sets — cardinality questions hinge on reading the notation correctly.

## Normal forms in one breath

1. **1NF** — atomic values, no repeating groups.
2. **2NF** — 1NF + no partial dependency on a composite key.
3. **3NF** — 2NF + no transitive dependency of non-key attributes on the key.
4. **BCNF** — every determinant is a candidate key.

<Callout type="tip">
TwoNF removes *partial*; threeNF removes *transitive*. One word each — learn them as pairs.
</Callout>

## ACID

- **Atomicity** — all or nothing (rollback).
- **Consistency** — constraints hold before and after.
- **Isolation** — concurrent transactions don't interfere.
- **Durability** — committed data survives crashes (WAL/force policy).

## Indexing

- **Primary index** on ordered file, dense/sparse.
- **Secondary index** on non-key or unordered attribute — extra indirection level.
- **B+ tree** keeps data only at leaves → more keys per node, better range scans.

<Callout type="info">
"Which index is best for range queries on a sorted key?" → primary/B+ tree ordered index. Equality queries also do well with hashing.
</Callout>

## Concurrency

Conflict serializability via precedence graph; two-phase locking (2PL) guarantees it but can deadlock. Timestamp ordering and MVCC are the alternatives name-dropped in harder papers.
